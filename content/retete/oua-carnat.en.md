This routine was already right before the plan existed. It's cheap, it's fast, it has the fat
that starts the day properly, and it demands no decisions at seven in the morning.

**Cabbage took the potatoes' place.** Same job — something to fry in the fat running out of the
sausage, something to fill the pan — at a fifth of the carbohydrate and ten cents a portion.
The missing calories came from butter, which you don't see on the plate.

If it starts to bore you, eighty grams of mushrooms do exactly the same job and are better with
eggs. They cost four times as much, though, so save them for the weekend.

**The one thing worth checking: the sausage label.** Sausages are the only processed food in the
whole week, and two things hide in there that matter:

- **Sodium.** Fifty-five grams of sausage bring about 440 mg of sodium — a quarter of your day,
  from the smallest thing on the plate. That's why no salt goes over the eggs.
- **Added phosphates** (E338–E452 on the label). Phosphorus from meat is about half absorbed;
  the kind from additives is absorbed almost completely. Look for ">85% pork" on the label and
  none of those E numbers.

The very cheapest sausages also contain rusk or flour as filler. Those don't belong in the plan.

**No salt over the eggs.** Seriously. Taste first. The sausage seasons the whole pan, including
the eggs and the cabbage next to it.
