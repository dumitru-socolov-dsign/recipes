  - text: "Oven on at 200°C. Cut the courgette into rounds a centimetre thick and put it in the tray with half the olive oil."
  - text: "Melt 60 g of butter in a bowl. Add the crushed garlic, the zest and juice of one lemon, and the pepper."
  - text: "Sit the salmon on baking paper over the courgette, skin down. Pour the lemon butter over it. Don't salt it."

Once a week, and only once. Salmon is the most expensive protein on the list — but its omega-3
fats are the one thing none of the other recipes can provide.

**The only meal of the week with less meat, and that's deliberate.** A hundred and twenty grams
of salmon per portion is less than you get with the pork or the chicken, because salmon costs
three times as much per kilo and has the densest protein on the list. The missing calories come
from butter and olive oil, not from fish. That keeps it at five euro a portion instead of nine.

**The walnuts are there for contrast, not calories.** Twenty grams, toasted, give the crunch
that a plate of fish and soft vegetables otherwise lacks. More would be pointless: walnuts
carry the most phosphorus in the whole plan, and this meal is already fish.

**The alternative at an eighth of the price:** two tins of mackerel in oil have the same omega-3.
If money is tight this week, [the mackerel](/en/recipes/mackerel-onion/) does the same job.
