The recipe that keeps the budget in check. Two tins of mackerel cost a fifth of what a piece of
salmon does and bring more omega-3 per euro than anything else in the shop.

**Tinned mackerel isn't a compromise.** The fish is canned within hours of being caught, so its
fats are fresher than those of a fillet that has sat on a shelf for three days. The one real
drawback is sodium: 350 mg per hundred grams. That's why the recipe carries only a gram of salt
across two portions.

**Courgette and cabbage, in the pan that's already going.** Both brown quickly over high heat and
soak up the butter. They're there for bulk: together they bring under 120 calories, while the
other two thousand come from butter, lard and the oil in the tins.

**Vinegar off the heat.** The acidity cuts through the fat of the fish and makes it taste lighter.
Added over high heat it evaporates completely and nothing is left of it.
