The best cost-per-portion in the whole plan: three meals from a single tray and twenty
minutes of hands-on work.

**Pork belly, not butter.** The old version of this recipe had 195 g of butter over the tray —
1400 calories you couldn't see on the plate. Those calories now come from 440 g of cubed pork
belly that browns and is visibly there. It costs about the same per calorie, but you end up
with 230 g of meat per portion instead of 133.

**Twenty percent mince, not five.** It's cheaper and it has the fat that keeps the meatballs
juicy. Lean mince in the oven comes out dry and needs oil anyway — better to pay less and get
the fat for free.

**No breadcrumbs, no flour, no potatoes.** Grated onion is the binder: the water in it binds
with the protein in the meat and keeps the meatballs soft. Breadcrumbs are only there to
stretch the meat and cut the cost.

**Cauliflower and broccoli, not root vegetables.** Same job on the plate — bulk and something
that browns — at a fifth of the carbohydrate. Frozen costs half what fresh does and doesn't
spoil if the cooking session slips a day.

**The butter that's left is only for flavour.** Forty grams at the end, just enough to bind
the vegetables with the fat that ran out of the pork. The rest of the calories are already in
the tray, in the form of meat.
