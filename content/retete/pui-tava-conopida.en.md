This one is the backbone of the week: the cheapest meat in the shop and the oven doing all
the work.

**Two meats, two jobs.** The thighs bring the roast flavour and the protein; the pork belly
strips bring the calories, at half the protein per gram. Together that's over half a kilo of
meat for two portions — 260 g each — without pushing the day's protein up.

**Why bone-in, skin-on thighs rather than chicken breast.** Breast costs three times as much
per kilo of protein, dries out if you forget it for two minutes and has no fat. Thighs forgive
you, and as they render they season the cauliflower underneath. The bone keeps the meat juicy.

**Frozen cauliflower, not fresh.** Half the price, already cut, and it doesn't rot in the
fridge. Its only problem is water, and water is solved with plenty of fat and a hot oven —
which is why the lard goes in from the start and the oven is at 220°C, not 180.

**Why smoked paprika.** It gives the impression of smoke and salt without adding any salt.
It's the most efficient thing on the spice shelf for low-sodium cooking.

**Butter at the end, not the start.** Put it in at the beginning and it burns over 40 minutes
at 220°C. Added at the end it stays butter — you taste it. There's far less of it than in the
old version of this recipe, because those calories now come from pork belly, which is a great
deal more satisfying on the plate than a melted knob of butter.
