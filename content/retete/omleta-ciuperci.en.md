This isn't a recipe from the plan, it's a safety net. When the cooking session got skipped or
the food ran out early, this gets you out of trouble with what's already in the house.

**Three eggs and a hundred grams of butter.** The butter is the main ingredient here, not the
frying fat: it brings 700 of the meal's 1200 calories. The eggs are for structure and the
mushrooms for bulk. Use 20 g of butter and you have a snack, not a dinner.

**Mushrooms, not potatoes.** Same job — something that fills the pan and browns — with 2 g of
carbohydrate per hundred instead of 17. Browned properly they also bring umami, which is why
this omelette needs under a gram of salt.

**No milk in the eggs.** This myth ruins more omelettes than anything else. Milk makes the
proteins set unevenly and leaves water in the pan. Eggs beaten plain, with plenty of fat and a
low heat, come out creamy on their own.
